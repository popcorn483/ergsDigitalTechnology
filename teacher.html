      Topic worksheet

      A compact worksheet for this topic: key terms from the workbook glossary, the workbook’s exam tips, then CCEA past-paper questions (the first ones in the list above, in its order; untick any you don’t want). Answers gives the key-term answers and the mark schemes.

      About the papers and reports
      • To add an examiner report, upload CCEA’s Chief Examiner’s report PDF to papers/GCSE/Examiner reports/ or papers/A level/Examiner reports/ in the GitHub repo, named after the series, e.g. Summer 2027.pdf. A GitHub Action finds each question’s comment a minute or two later.
      • Where a report discusses a whole question in one paragraph, the pack shows that paragraph with the lines about the part highlighted.
      • CCEA publishes no report for the 2021 series, the specimen papers or the 2022 A2 1 resit, so those questions have no report section.
      • The papers, mark schemes and examiner reports are CCEA material, used with permission, cut straight from CCEA’s PDFs (the edge watermark is trimmed off). Packs are made in your browser; nothing is uploaded.

      Build a test

      A mock or end-of-unit test from CCEA’s past papers. Pick the units and topics and set the marks or the time; the questions are spread across the topics (as often as CCEA asks each one, or evenly), mix short and longer questions and hit the marks. Check the list, swap or remove any question, then download the test paper, the mark schemes and examiner comments, or both together.

          Exam trends

          Every question in CCEA’s past papers, as the workbooks place them, totalled by topic and paper. Open a topic for its spec points and the questions on each, or to make its topic pack.

          • Each question counts once per paper, with the marks the workbook gives it, or else the marks printed on the paper.
          • A question the workbook places in two topics has its marks split equally between them, so each paper’s topics add up to the paper’s total. On the spec points, a question on two pages of a topic is split between them too.
          • Papers: the specimen, every summer series and the 2022 A2 1 resit. “Last” is the last summer it was asked (not the specimen). Due: not asked in the last two summer papers.
          • Totals are rounded to whole marks.

            Scheme of work

            Each unit in teaching order: its topics, the lessons for each one, and a rough number of weeks. Topic names open the pupil workbook.

            Weeks = lessons × periods per lesson ÷ periods a week, rounded to the nearest half week (a workbook-only topic counts one lesson per two workbook sections). Coursework units have no weeks: they run to your own deadlines. AS 1 and AS 2 run side by side, so share the A level periods between them. The exam line on each unit compares the weeks left with the scheme’s weeks.

            KS3 topics

            Choose which topics Year 8, 9 and 10 learn, and in what order. Every topic lives in the topic library, so a topic you take out of a year (Web Design, say) is kept, with its files and notes, for another year.

            • Swap replaces a topic with one from the library, or a new one. Remove puts it back in the library. A topic picked from another year moves across.
            • Build a new topic: give it a title, a summary and what pupils will learn, and add its PowerPoints and other files. It shows in the year book straight away with a “notes on the way” page and its PowerPoint. Then ask Claude to write its notes from the files: the topic’s folder is shown on its card.
            • Save to the site sends every change to GitHub in one go. The books rebuild in about 3 to 5 minutes (longer when new PowerPoints need their PDF copies).
            • Pupils’ progress is kept by position (Year 9 topic 2), so swap or reorder topics between school years, before pupils start them.

            Pupil tracking

            Pupils must enter their first name, surname, form class and a 4-digit PIN before using the Revision Guide (they can change it under About on the home page). If that name and form class are already in your sheet and the PIN matches, the device joins that pupil and pulls in their saved progress (five wrong PINs locks it for 15 minutes; Reset PIN lets them choose a new one). PINs are stored only as a scrambled hash. Their active time is then sent per page, along with each workbook’s pages covered and opened, best chapter-quiz scores and trophies, and their progress is saved online under their name. Names are not checked. Devices that have opened the Teacher area are never asked and never counted.

            Classes & zones: add each class with its teacher; pupils then pick their class from a list when they sign in, and the Teacher filter shows just your classes. Zones are the units and years a pupil can open (a class’s zones, or a pupil’s own, which can be several at once). Anything else shows a lock and a button to ask you; requests appear here to open or decline.

            The data is in your Google Sheet (the Log tab). Your key stays on this device.
